Tensor → Sensation

Ordinary variance, mistaken for threat

An institution's nociceptive threshold can drift downward the same way a nervous system's can. Institutional allodynia is the condition in which routine disagreement, minor budget shortfall, or ordinary personnel turnover is processed as an existential emergency — triggering the resource-allocation switch that should be reserved for genuine threats to survival.

The distinction that matters is not whether a disturbance occurred, but whether it exceeds expected variance — the institution's own learned model of normal turbulence. A healthy system distinguishes signal from noise. A sensitized one has forgotten what noise sounds like.

Every stimulus produces two very different objective functions depending on which mode is active: maximize long-run efficiency (normal operation) versus guarantee immediate survival (existential mode). The label "existential" is what flips the switch — and that label can be applied correctly or falsely.

Matrix → Attention

The escalation threshold

An institution — like a nervous system — implicitly runs a volatility model. Escalation from ordinary adaptation to emergency mode is warranted only when the observed mismatch clears a learned threshold, τ:

Escalate if   ( Observed Mismatch / Expected Variance )  >  τ

where Expected Variance is the institution's own "GARCH" — its learned estimate of normal turbulence,
and τ is the threshold for invoking emergency powers or committing extraordinary resources.

Sensitization is what happens when the denominator collapses toward zero: every residual then appears enormous, and low-grade stimuli begin tripping high-grade responses.

Institutions that spent selectively, not uniformly

Durable institutions survived not by avoiding mistakes but by developing mechanisms that treated most disturbances as adaptable noise, reserving scarce resources — capital, human talent, legitimacy, attention, political capital, optionality — for genuine threats.

InstitutionAdaptationResource conserved
Roman EmpireIncorporated former enemies via citizenship rather than annihilationMilitary capital
Catholic ChurchSeparated negotiable doctrine from essential core across centuriesLegitimacy / continuity
University of BolognaDistributed governance and patronage rather than tying to one rulerOptionality

Each case is the same move at different scale: partition reality into a core that must be defended and a periphery that can adapt — so that no single perturbation propagates into the institution's deepest identity.

Eigenmode → Reaction

Five mechanisms of institutional sensitization

How does an ancient, once-resilient system end up with a pathologically low threshold?

01

Success changes the training data

Young institutions are selected by reality; old ones by memory. A survived catastrophe becomes an inherited scar — the threshold never fully resets.

02

Incentives reward false positives

The cost of missing a crisis is visible; the cost of repeated overreaction is diffuse. Threat amplification gets selected for.

03

Bureaucracies remember, rarely forget

Every crisis leaves a committee, a policy, a reporting layer. Very few crises retire old rules. Sensitivity ratchets upward.

04

Loss of variance estimation

The system forgets what normal variability looks like — as if it assumes σ²→0 — so every residual reads as exceptional.

05

Identity fuses with defense

"Is this true?" becomes "Is this dangerous to us?" Once identity and survival couple tightly, criticism itself is felt as injury.
